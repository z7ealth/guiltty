#+TITLE: The visualizer: bars that move with the music
#+STARTUP: overview

* The idea

While something plays (Spotify, a video in the browser, =mpv=),
sixteen little bars at the right end of the tab bar rise and fall with
it: bass on the left, treble on the right. When the sound stops, the
bars sink and guiltty stops redrawing for them.

#+begin_example
   one  two                         ▂▄▆█▇▅▃▂▃▅▄▂▁        search: 3 of 7
  └── tabs ───┘                    └── 16 bands ──┘      └── status ──┘
#+end_example

It's on by default. Two things to know about it: while it's on, guiltty
records what your speakers play (never the microphone, but it is a
recording), and the bar is redrawn up to 30 times a second while sound
plays. Silence costs nothing (see "Silence costs nothing" below). To turn
it off:

#+begin_src elixir
visualizer: false
#+end_src

* What sound is, to a program

** Samples

Sound reaches a program as /samples/: the speaker cone's position,
measured tens of thousands of times a second. guiltty asks for 48,000
a second (48 kHz), as 32-bit floats from -1.0 to 1.0, two channels
(left, right) interleaved:

#+begin_example
  L0  R0  L1  R1  L2  R2 ...        each a float, 4 bytes
  └frame┘ └frame┘ └frame┘           1 frame = 1/48000 s
#+end_example

A pure tone of 1000 Hz is a sine wave: the samples go up and down 1000
times a second. Music is thousands of such waves added together.

** From time to frequencies: the FFT

The bars need the opposite view: not "where is the cone now" but "how
much of each frequency is in this moment". That's what the /Fourier
transform/ gives. Take N samples, and it returns N/2 useful numbers,
/bins/: bin k is how strong the frequency k × (rate / N) is.

guiltty takes N = 2048 samples (43 ms of sound), so the bins are
48000 / 2048 = 23.4 Hz apart: bin 1 is 23.4 Hz, bin 43 is about
1 kHz, bin 1023 is 24 kHz.

Computed directly, that's N² multiplications (4 million) per slice. The
/FFT/ (fast Fourier transform, Cooley and Tukey's radix-2 version) gets
the same answer in N log₂ N (22,000): it splits the slice into its
even and odd samples, transforms each half (recursively), and combines
the two with one /butterfly/ per pair. guiltty's FFT is 30 lines of C.

#+begin_example
  2048 samples ──► window ──► FFT ──► 1024 bins ──► 16 bands ──► 16 bytes
  (43 ms)          (Hann)            (23.4 Hz      (log spaced,   (0..255)
                                       apart)        in dB)
#+end_example

** The window

Cutting 2048 samples out of a song makes a slice with sudden edges, and
a sudden edge is, to the FFT, a click: a little of /every/ frequency.
That's /spectral leakage/: a pure 1 kHz tone would light up every bar.
The fix is to fade the slice in and out first: multiply it by a /Hann
window/, a smooth hump that's 0 at both ends and 1 in the middle.

** Bands: what ears hear

Ears hear pitch by ratios: 100 to 200 Hz sounds as far apart as 1000 to
2000 Hz (an octave each). Sixteen bars of equal width in Hz would give
the treble fifteen of them. So the bands are spaced evenly in /octaves/:
50 Hz to 16 kHz in 16 steps, about half an octave each.

| Band | From (Hz) | To (Hz) |   | Band | From (Hz) | To (Hz) |
|------+-----------+---------+---+------+-----------+---------|
|    0 |        50 |      72 |   |    8 |       894 |    1283 |
|    1 |        72 |     103 |   |    9 |      1283 |    1839 |
|    2 |       103 |     147 |   |   10 |      1839 |    2638 |
|    3 |       147 |     211 |   |   11 |      2638 |    3783 |
|    4 |       211 |     303 |   |   12 |      3783 |    5425 |
|    5 |       303 |     435 |   |   13 |      5425 |    7780 |
|    6 |       435 |     624 |   |   14 |      7780 |   11157 |
|    7 |       624 |     894 |   |   15 |     11157 |   16000 |

A band takes the bins whose frequency is inside it (=ceil= of both
edges divided by 23.4), and its strength is its loudest bin's. The low
bands get one bin each: at 23.4 Hz apart, that's all there is.

** Loudness: decibels

Ears hear loudness by ratios too, so the strength becomes decibels:
=20 log₁₀(amplitude)=, where a full-scale sine is 0 dB and each halving
is about -6 dB. Then:

- *tilt*: music has less energy the higher it goes (about 3 dB less per
  octave), so each band is lifted 3 dB per octave above 1 kHz (and
  lowered below). Without that, every song's bars slope down to the
  right.
- *range*: -62 dB is an empty bar, -12 dB a full one.
- *fall*: a bar jumps up at once but sinks by at most a tenth of its
  height per frame, so it doesn't flicker. That's what makes it look
  like a meter.

The result is a byte per band, 0 to 255.

* Where the sound comes from: the sink's monitor

On a Linux desktop today, sound goes through *PipeWire*: a server that
holds a graph of /nodes/ (programs playing, speakers, microphones)
linked port to port. Your speakers are a /sink/, and every sink has a
/monitor/: its output, offered again to anyone who wants to record it.

guiltty connects a capture stream with the property
=stream.capture.sink = true=, which tells PipeWire "record what the
default sink plays", rather than the default microphone:

#+begin_example
  Spotify ──► ┌──────────────┐ ──► speakers
  Firefox ──► │ default sink │
              └──────┬───────┘
                     │ monitor
                     ▼
            guiltty-visualizer        (a capture stream: passive)
#+end_example

It's also /passive/ (=node.passive = true=): this stream alone doesn't
keep the sink running. When nothing plays, the sink suspends as it
would without guiltty, so the helper receives no buffers and does
nothing. You can see the stream with =wpctl status= or =pw-top= while
it runs.

guiltty asks for float, stereo, 48 kHz; PipeWire converts whatever the
sink plays into that (=SPA_AUDIO_FORMAT_F32= in the format it offers).

* The parts

** Process tree

#+begin_example
  Guiltty.Supervisor (rest_for_one)
  ├── Guiltty.Registry
  ├── Guiltty.PubSub          Registry, duplicate keys: who listens
  ├── ...
  ├── Guiltty.Watchdog
  ├── Guiltty.Audio ──port──► guiltty_audio        (only while on)
  │                              └── PipeWire thread
  └── (Task)

  Guiltty.Desks
  └── Desk ─ Window ──port──► guiltty_vk           (one per window)
#+end_example

Three things to notice:

- *Recording is a program of its own*, =priv/guiltty_audio=, not part
  of the renderer and not a NIF. The renderer is one per window, and
  the sound is one per machine: one helper serves every window. And the
  rules say C in the BEAM is two NIFs, nothing more.
- *Audio comes late in the supervisor*. Under =rest_for_one= a crash
  restarts the crashed child and everything after it. With Audio near
  the end, a crash there never takes a window down.
- *Windows don't register with Audio itself.* They register in
  =Guiltty.PubSub=, a =Registry= with duplicate keys, under =:audio=.
  The registry belongs to the application, so a window's registration
  outlives a restart of Audio, and goes away by itself when the window
  ends (the registry monitors it). Audio sends with
  =Registry.dispatch/3=.

** The data flow, 30 times a second

#+begin_example
  PipeWire thread        guiltty_audio      Guiltty.Audio      Window          renderer
  (in guiltty_audio)     (on that thread)
       │ process()            │                  │                │               │
       ├─ buffer of frames ──►│                  │                │               │
       │                      │ mono, ring       │                │               │
       │                      │ every 1600       │                │               │
       │                      │ frames: FFT      │                │               │
       │                      ├── LEVELS ───────►│                │               │
       │                      │  (stdout)        ├─ {:audio, b} ─►│               │
       │                      │                  │  (dispatch)    ├── AUDIO ─────►│
       │                      │                  │                │  (port)       │ tab bar:
       │                      │                  │                │               │ row 0 redrawn
#+end_example

1600 frames at 48 kHz is 1/30 of a second: that's the rate. Each
analysis looks at the last 2048, so slices overlap a little, which
keeps the bars smooth.

** Silence costs nothing

When every band is 0, the helper sends one all-zero LEVELS (the bars
empty) and then nothing until there's sound again. Elixir doesn't
filter anything; the renderer doesn't redraw. The rate is "30 a second
while sound plays", not "30 a second".

** When the sound stops, nothing arrives

The fall above runs once per hop of samples. So it needs samples, and
when a song is paused or its browser tab closed, there are none:
PipeWire stops running the graph for a sink with no stream playing into
it, and a passive capture like ours isn't sent silence, it's sent
nothing. The bars froze where they were.

So the helper has a timer, added to PipeWire's own loop
(=pw_loop_add_timer=, a =timerfd(2)= underneath), on the same thread
that analyzes, so nothing needs a lock. It's armed only while the bars
are up, and ticks once per hop (33 ms). On a tick with no buffer for
150 ms, it feeds a hop of zeros, exactly as if PipeWire had: the bars
fall in about ten steps, the all-zero LEVELS goes out, and the timer is
disarmed. 150 ms, because buffers can come several hops apart (a large
quantum) and a tick between two of them must not count as silence.

Real messages, a 0.3 s tone played with =pw-play= and then nothing (the
loudest bar of each LEVELS):

#+begin_example
  without the timer   H 37 105 105 105 105 105 105 105 105          stuck at 105
  with it             H 37 105 105 105 105 105 105 105 105 93 67 42 16 0
#+end_example

* The messages

** guiltty_audio → Guiltty.Audio

Framed like every port here, ={:packet, 4}=: a 4-byte big-endian length,
then the body.

#+begin_example
  HELLO    u8 1, u16 version              (once, on start)
  LEVELS   u8 2, u8 count, count bytes    (lowest band first)
#+end_example

Real bytes, from =guiltty_audio --raw= on a second of a 1 kHz tone
(=xxd=):

#+begin_example
  00000000: 0000 0003 0100 01                       HELLO, version 1
  00000007: 00 0000 1202 1000 050e 1720 2d41 79ff   LEVELS, 16 bands: the first slice
            5725 0500 0000 0000 0000                is half silence, half tone:
                                                    leakage everywhere, peak in band 8
  ...
  00000288: 00 0000 1202 1000 0000 0000 0000 0014   a second in: band 8 (0xff) and a
            ff00 0000 0000 0000 00                  trace in band 7 (0x14); the rest 0
#+end_example

The first slice shows what the window is for: the sudden start of the
tone is an edge, and an edge has every frequency. Once the slice is all
tone, only band 8 (894 to 1283 Hz) is left, with a little of its
neighbour.

** Guiltty.Audio → Window → renderer

=Window= wraps the levels in a renderer message, protocol version 16:

#+begin_example
  AUDIO    u8 29, u8 count (at most 32; 0 hides the bars), count bytes
#+end_example

#+begin_src elixir
iex> Guiltty.Window.Protocol.audio(<<0, 128, 255>>)
<<29, 3, 0, 128, 255>>
#+end_src

* Drawing the bars

The tab bar is cells (chapter 25), so the bars are cells too: one per
band, each one of the /block elements/ =▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█= (U+2581 to U+2588,
one eighth to eight eighths of a cell), or a space for silence. That's
nine heights per bar: =level × 9 / 256=. Those glyphs are drawn by
guiltty itself as sprites (chapter 14), so they fill the cell exactly
in any font, and both backends already draw them. No shader, no new
backend code.

They're in the accent color, at the right end of the bar, just before
the status, with a space on each side (only before them when nothing
follows). The right end of the bar, the bars and the status, is drawn
against the window's right edge, past the padding (=bar_right=,
[[file:06-renderer.org][chapter 6]]), so with no status the last bar
touches the edge. Their room is kept before the
tabs are placed, so any number of tabs shrink or scroll around them
(chapter 25, "Too many tabs"); only a window so narrow that the tabs
would have fewer than 16 columns hides them.

That room is fixed for as long as the visualizer is on, sound or not,
so the tabs don't change width when a song starts or stops. The
renderer only knows "bars" (a count above 0) or "no bars" (AUDIO with
count 0), so =Guiltty.Audio= sends the bars at rest, 16 zeros, as soon
as the helper says HELLO, and the empty message only when the helper
stops (the setting went off, or no PipeWire). In between, silence is
the helper's last all-zero LEVELS, which keeps the room too.

Two windows can miss that first message: one opened later, and one
whose renderer hasn't said hello yet (or was replaced by the OpenGL
fallback). So =Audio= remembers what it last sent and tells a new
listener at once (=listen/0= casts it =:tell=), and =Window= keeps the
last levels in its state and sends them right after the handshake.

#+begin_example
  helper   HELLO ── LEVELS … LEVELS(all 0) ─ (silence: nothing) ─ LEVELS … ── exit
  Audio    16×0  ── levels … 16×0                                levels … ── <<>>
  bar      ▁▁▁▁  ── ▃▆█▅ …   ▁▁▁▁   (room kept the whole time)    ▂▇▄▃ …   ── no room
#+end_example

Each
AUDIO rebuilds row 0 and marks it dirty; the other rows aren't sent to
the GPU again.

* When it can't run

=guiltty_audio= loads PipeWire with =dlopen(3)= instead of linking it,
so it starts on any machine, and so the precompiled release still needs
only libc and libm from it (chapter 17). How it ends tells
=Guiltty.Audio= what happened:

| Exit status | Means                                          | Guiltty.Audio                                     |
|-------------+------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
|           0 | stdin closed (the port was closed)             | expected when it stops it; otherwise tries again   |
|           2 | no =libpipewire-0.3.so.0=                      | a notice in the log; no retry until off and on    |
|           3 | PipeWire there, but wouldn't record            | a warning; tries again in 10 s                    |

Either way a window just shows no bars: nothing else depends on it.

The helper stops when its stdin closes. That's how an Erlang port says
goodbye: =Port.close/1= closes the pipe, =read(2)= returns 0, and the
helper stops PipeWire's thread and exits. If guiltty itself dies, the
same pipe closes, so a helper is never left behind.

* Trying it without guiltty

#+begin_src sh
./priv/guiltty_audio --print     # play something; Ctrl+C to stop
#+end_src

draws the bands in your terminal, on one line redrawn in place. And
=--raw FILE= analyzes a file of float stereo samples instead of
recording, as fast as it can read. That's what =test/guiltty/audio_test.exs=
does: it writes one second of a sine wave and checks the right band is
the loudest (100 Hz in band 1, 1 kHz in band 8, 8 kHz in band 14).

* Questions

1. Why does guiltty record the sink's /monitor/ and not a source?
2. A song has a strong 100 Hz kick drum. Which bar moves, and why
   does it get only one FFT bin?
3. Without the Hann window, what would a pure 1 kHz tone look like on
   the bars?
4. Why is =guiltty_audio= a program of its own, and not part of the
   renderer?
5. If =Guiltty.Audio= crashes, what happens to your windows? What if it
   came right after =Guiltty.Config= in the supervisor?
6. Nothing is playing. How many messages per second go from the
   helper to Elixir? From Elixir to each renderer?
7. Why =dlopen= PipeWire instead of linking =-lpipewire-0.3=?
8. You open a new window while nothing plays. The helper said HELLO
   minutes ago. How does the new window know to leave room for the bars?
9. You pause a song. Why don't the bars just fall by themselves, and
   what makes them fall?

** Answers
:answers:
1. A source is an input (a microphone). The monitor is the sink's
   output, what the speakers play, offered again for recording;
   =stream.capture.sink= asks for the default sink's monitor.
2. Band 1 (72 to 103 Hz). Bins are 23.4 Hz apart and the band is only
   31 Hz wide, so just one bin (93.75 Hz) falls inside it. A finer
   bass needs a longer slice (more samples, more delay).
3. Every bar would show something: the slice's sudden edges are clicks,
   and a click contains every frequency (spectral leakage). With the
   window, only band 8 lights up, and a trace of band 7.
4. The renderer runs once per window and sound is once per machine; and
   C inside the BEAM is two NIFs, nothing more. As a port program it
   can also crash or fail to find PipeWire without touching anything
   else.
5. Nothing: under =rest_for_one= only Audio and the children after it
   restart, and none of those is a window. A window's =:audio=
   registration lives in =Guiltty.PubSub=, so the bars keep coming
   after the restart. Right after Config, its crash would restart
   Sessions and Desks: every tab and window.
6. None, after one all-zero LEVELS that empties the bars (they keep
   their room: the renderer still has 16 bars, all at rest). And once the
   sink suspends, a few seconds later (the stream is passive, so it
   doesn't hold it awake), the helper isn't even woken up.
7. So guiltty runs on a machine without PipeWire (it just has no
   bars), and the precompiled release needs nothing beyond libc and
   libm from it, like SDL's own audio backends.
8. Its =Audio.listen/0= casts =:tell= to =Guiltty.Audio=, which sends
   it the levels it last sent everyone: 16 zeros. The window keeps them
   and sends them to its renderer right after the handshake.
9. The fall runs on each hop of samples, and a paused stream means
   PipeWire sends none, not even silence. The helper's idle timer
   notices no buffer for 150 ms and feeds it hops of zeros until the
   bars reach 0.
:END:
