#+TITLE: Multi window: every window its own desk
#+STARTUP: overview

* What changed in v0.13.0

Until v0.13.0 guiltty had one window. Running =guiltty= again only raised
it, and every tab belonged to it. Now there can be as many windows as you
like, and each is *independent*: its own tabs, its own active tab, its own
locked tabs, its own broadcast group. Closing one never touches another,
and a crash in one never reaches another.

| You do                               | guiltty does                                   |
|--------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| =guiltty= (or the menu), not running | starts, with one window                        |
| =guiltty= while running              | brings back a closed window that kept locked tabs, or else opens a new one |
| Ctrl+Shift+N in a window             | opens a new window, always fresh               |
| close a window                       | closes its tabs, except the locked ones        |
| close the last window (none locked)  | guiltty stops                                  |

* A desk per window

The heart of it is one small supervisor per window, a *desk*
([[file:../lib/guiltty/desk.ex][Guiltty.Desk]]), holding that window's =Tabs= and its =Window=:

#+begin_example
  Guiltty.Supervisor (rest_for_one)
  |-- Guiltty.Registry
  |-- Guiltty.Config
  |-- Guiltty.Sessions          every tab's session, of every window
  |-- Guiltty.Desks             DynamicSupervisor
  |   |-- Desk 1                rest_for_one, auto_shutdown: any_significant
  |   |   |-- Tabs              significant: its last tab closing ends the desk
  |   |   `-- Window            the renderer
  |   `-- Desk 3
  |       |-- Tabs
  |       `-- Window
  |-- Guiltty.Lifeline          significant: the last desk ending stops guiltty
  `-- (a Task)                  the first window, at boot
#+end_example

Why a supervisor, and why this order:

- *rest_for_one, Tabs first.* The Window shows what its Tabs says. If the
  Tabs restarts, the Window restarts after it and attaches to the new
  one. If only the Window crashes (its renderer died), it restarts alone.
- *Tabs is significant.* When a desk's last tab closes, its Tabs stops, and
  =auto_shutdown: :any_significant= ends the desk with it.
- *The Window isn't.* Closing a window with locked tabs leaves the desk
  running without one: the tabs keep going.

The sessions don't live in the desk: they stay under =Guiltty.Sessions=,
so a desk can crash and restart without killing a single shell.

* Whose tabs are these?

With one =Tabs= there was nothing to ask: every session was its tab. Now
each desk must recognize its own, also after a crash, when its new =Tabs=
starts with an empty list. The answer is in the Registry. Every session's
supervisor is registered under ={id, :supervisor}=, and Registry keys can
carry a *value*: the desk's id. This is the real Registry with two
windows, after a new tab in the first:

#+begin_example
  {{2, :supervisor}, 1}       session 2 belongs to desk 1
  {{4, :supervisor}, 3}       session 4 belongs to desk 3
  {{5, :supervisor}, 1}       session 5 belongs to desk 1
  {{:desk, 1}, nil}           the desks' own names
  {{:desk, 3}, nil}
  {{:desk, 1, :tabs}, nil}    and their Tabs
  {{:desk, 3, :tabs}, nil}
#+end_example

A restarted =Tabs= asks for the sessions whose value is its desk, with a
match spec ([[file:../lib/guiltty/tabs.ex::defp running_sessions][running_sessions/1]]):

#+begin_src elixir
Registry.select(Guiltty.Registry, [
  {{{:"$1", :supervisor}, :"$2", :"$3"}, [{:is_integer, :"$1"}, {:"=:=", :"$3", desk}],
   [{{:"$1", :"$2"}}]}
])
#+end_src

Ids now come from =System.unique_integer([:positive, :monotonic])=: unique
across every desk (the Registry names sessions by id, so two desks can't
both have a "tab 1") and increasing, so sorting them gives the order the
tabs were opened. You can see it above: desks and sessions share one
counter (1, 2, 3, 4, 5).

* Lifeline: when does guiltty stop?

"guiltty stops when the last tab closes" used to mean: =Tabs= is
significant in the application's supervisor. With many =Tabs= that can't
work: the first window's last tab would stop everything. A desk can't be
significant either, because desks live under a DynamicSupervisor, which
has no =auto_shutdown=.

So [[file:../lib/guiltty/lifeline.ex][Guiltty.Lifeline]] watches the desks. =Lifeline.open/1= starts a
desk and monitors it. When a desk ends normally, it's crossed off; when
the last is crossed off, Lifeline stops, and since /it/ is significant,
guiltty stops. A desk that *crashes* isn't counted as gone: =Desks=
restarts it under the same name, and Lifeline finds the new one.

#+begin_example
  window 1 closed (no locks)  -> Tabs 1 ends -> Desk 1 ends -> Lifeline: 1 left
  window 3 closed (no locks)  -> Tabs 3 ends -> Desk 3 ends -> Lifeline: none
                                                             -> stops -> guiltty stops
#+end_example

* Closing a window that has locked tabs

#+begin_example
  Desk 1: Tabs [2, 5🔒], Window
     | the user closes the window: renderer exits 0
     v
  Window: Tabs.close_unlocked -> tab 2 closes; Window stops (normally)
  Desk 1: Tabs [5🔒], no Window            <- "windowless"
     | later: $ guiltty
     v
  Lifeline.open: a windowless desk? Desk 1 -> Desk.reopen(1, display)
  Desk 1: Tabs [5🔒], a new Window -> attach -> tab 5, as you left it
#+end_example

A desk is windowless when its Window child is there but not running
(=Supervisor.which_children= says =:undefined=). =Desk.reopen/2= replaces
that child rather than restarting it, because the new window may be on
another display: the old child spec has the old =DISPLAY=.

The launcher reopens windowless desks first, the oldest one each time,
before opening any new window. Ctrl+Shift+N doesn't: it asks for a fresh
desk (=fresh: true=), because inside a window you asked for a new one.

* What stays shared

Independent doesn't mean separate programs. One BEAM runs every window,
and these are shared on purpose:

- the config (=Guiltty.Config=): a change applies to every window;
- the fallback to OpenGL: once Vulkan has failed, later windows start
  with OpenGL;
- the sessions' supervisor, so a desk's crash doesn't take its shells.

Each window still has its own renderer process, so a renderer crash takes
down only its own window.

* Loot

- =Supervisor=: =auto_shutdown= and =significant= children; why a
  =DynamicSupervisor= can't have them.
- =Supervisor.which_children/1=, =delete_child/2=, =start_child/2=: changing
  a running supervisor's children.
- =Registry=: values stored with keys, and =Registry.select/2= with match
  specs (=ets:select/2='s match specification guide).
- =System.unique_integer/1=: =:positive=, =:monotonic=.
- =Process.monitor/1=: watching processes you don't own (Lifeline doesn't
  link to the desks).

* Questions

1. Why are the sessions outside the desks?
2. Why is =Tabs= before =Window= in a desk, and why =rest_for_one=?
3. Why can't a desk be significant, so guiltty stops with the last one?
4. How does a restarted =Tabs= know which sessions are its own?
5. Why does =Desk.reopen/2= replace the Window instead of restarting it?
6. Why does Ctrl+Shift+N skip windowless desks, when =guiltty= doesn't?

** Answers
:PROPERTIES:
:VISIBILITY: folded
:END:

1. So a desk can crash and restart without killing its shells: the new
   =Tabs= takes them back.
2. The Window depends on its Tabs: if the Tabs restarts, the Window must
   restart after it to attach to the new one. =rest_for_one= restarts a
   child and everything after it, but not what comes before.
3. Desks are children of a DynamicSupervisor, which doesn't support
   =auto_shutdown=. Lifeline, a plain child of the application's
   supervisor, can be significant, so it counts the desks instead.
4. Each session's supervisor is registered with its desk's id as the
   Registry value; =running_sessions/1= selects by that value.
5. The old child spec holds the old Window options, including the display
   it was opened on. A new launch may come from another display.
6. Inside a window, Ctrl+Shift+N means "another window", so a fresh one.
   The launcher reopens a closed window first, because that's how you
   get back to tabs you locked.
