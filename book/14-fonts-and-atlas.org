#+TITLE: Fonts and the atlas: every glyph, from any font
#+STARTUP: overview

* What changed

Until MVP 5 the renderer drew 95 glyphs, ASCII 32 to 126, all from one
font, into an atlas built once at startup. Now a cell can hold any
character or cluster (chapter 13), and no single font has them all:
Source Code Pro has no kanji, no emoji, no Nerd Font icons.

#+begin_example
  cell: glyph 0x1F600, wide
     |
     v
  atlas cache: "😀", width 2 ---- seen before? ----> slot 412 (and 413)
     |  no
     v
  fonts.c: which font has U+1F600?   primary (Source Code Pro)? no
                                     fonts already open?        no
                                     ask fontconfig ---------> Noto Color Emoji
     |
     v
  HarfBuzz shapes it, FreeType draws it (in color), shrunk to 2 cells
     |
     v
  written into slots 412-413; the backend uploads just that band of rows
#+end_example

* Font fallback with fontconfig

fontconfig is the Linux font database: it knows every installed font,
what characters each one covers (its *charset*), and your preferences
(=monospace= means Source Code Pro on this machine). [[file:../c_src/renderer/core/fonts.c][fonts.c]] asks it two
kinds of questions:

1. At startup, "the best match for this family name": the primary font.
   It also sets the cell size, as before: the advance of =M= by
   ascender + descender.
2. When a character isn't in any open font: "fonts like this family that
   have these characters". That's =FcFontSort= with an =FC_CHARSET= in the
   pattern. Its ranking weighs everything (family, style, coverage), so we
   still check that a candidate covers all the characters before loading
   it.

Up to 24 fonts stay open, so each fallback costs one query, ever. A
character no installed font has goes on a short "missing" list (so we
don't ask again) and draws as a hollow box, called *tofu*. On this machine,
kanji are tofu until a CJK font is installed
(=sudo zypper install noto-sans-cjk-fonts=).

** Emoji: color, and a preference

Should ⚡ be the emoji or a plain symbol from a text font? Unicode says
some characters are emoji by default, and U+FE0F asks for emoji, U+FE0E
for text. =wants_color/2= follows that, roughly: VS16, the emoji blocks
(U+1F000 to U+1FAFF), and the wide symbols around U+2300 to U+2BFF
prefer a font that has color glyphs (=FC_COLOR= in the query). Noto Color
Emoji wins those.

Color emoji fonts are odd fonts: Noto Color Emoji's glyphs are PNG
pictures drawn for one fixed size, 109 pixels per em (=fc-query= says
=pixelsize: 109=; they live in its CBDT table). FreeType can't scale them:
the renderer selects that size (=FT_Select_Size=) and FreeType returns
each glyph, with =FT_LOAD_COLOR=, as a BGRA bitmap with premultiplied
alpha. That's far bigger than a cell, so it gets shrunk to fit.

* Shaping with HarfBuzz

A cluster like 👍🏽 is two codepoints but one picture: the font has a
special glyph for "thumbs up, medium skin tone", and its rules (OpenType
GSUB tables) say when to use it. Applying those rules is *shaping*, and
HarfBuzz does it: give it UTF-8 and a font, get back glyph ids and
positions.

#+begin_example
  "👍🏽" (2 codepoints)  -- hb_shape -->  one glyph: the font's own
                                       "thumbs up, medium skin tone"
  "e\u0301"           -- hb_shape -->  two glyphs: e, then the accent,
                                       offset so it sits over the e
#+end_example

Every cluster goes through HarfBuzz, even plain letters: one code path,
and it runs once per distinct glyph, since the atlas caches the result. HarfBuzz reads the font through the FreeType face
(=hb_ft_font_create_referenced=), so both always agree on the size.

* Drawing a glyph into its cell

=fonts_render= draws the shaped glyphs on a scratch canvas at the font's
own size, with room around the pen (glyphs reach left of it, or past
their advance), then places the result in the cell:

- *Fits* (a text font at our size): copied one to one, centered across
  the cell or two (for the primary font's glyphs that's no shift at all),
  with the baselines lined up.
- *Too big* (an emoji bitmap, or a glyph much wider than its cells):
  shrunk to fit, keeping its shape, centered. Each target pixel is the
  average of the block of source pixels it covers.

The atlas stores straight-alpha RGBA: text glyphs as white with coverage
in alpha (the shader tints them with the cell's color), emoji with their
own colors. A cell gets the =CELL_COLOR_GLYPH= bit so the shader knows
which.

* Drawn, not taken from a font

Some characters are really geometry: block elements (█ ▀ ▌ ▖), shades
(░ ▒ ▓) and box-drawing lines (─ │ ┌ ┼ ━ ╭). A font's versions rarely
fill the cell exactly, so lines of neighboring cells leave gaps, and most
fonts draw shades as dot patterns. powerlevel10k puts =░▒▓= at the ends of
its prompt segments, and they showed up as dotted blocks. Ghostty, kitty
and Alacritty draw these themselves, and so does guiltty now
([[file:../c_src/renderer/core/sprites.c][sprites.c]]). The atlas asks =sprite_render= first; the fonts only get what
it doesn't know.

- *Blocks and shades* fill eighths of the cell. ░ ▒ ▓ are the whole cell
  at a quarter, half and three quarters alpha.
- *Lines*: each light or heavy box character is four *arms* from the
  center (up, right, down, left), each none, light or heavy. A table of
  76 entries covers =─= to =╋=, plus the half lines =╴= to =╿=. Arms are
  whole-pixel rectangles through the center pixel, the same pixel in every
  cell, so they meet the next cell exactly. Each arm reaches across the
  crossing stroke, so corners are square. Light is 1 pixel at 18 px, heavy
  2. (Double lines and dashes still come from the font.)
- *Rounded corners* (=╭ ╮ ╯ ╰=) are a quarter circle, anti-aliased by
  distance from the curve, ending on the same row and column as the lines.

=╭= at 11x23 pixels, alpha per pixel, rows 11 to 18 (=─= runs on row 11,
=│= on column 5):

#+begin_example
  11:   .   .   .   .   .   .   .  43 156 229 255
  12:   .   .   .   .   .   .  87 255 120  31   .
  13:   .   .   .   .   .  43 255  61   .   .   .
  14:   .   .   .   .   . 156 120   .   .   .   .
  15:   .   .   .   .   . 229  31   .   .   .   .
  16:   .   .   .   .   . 255   .   .   .   .   .
#+end_example

A first version anti-aliased the straight lines too. A 2-pixel heavy line
centered on a pixel's middle came out as one solid row between two
half-transparent ones, and looked like two thin lines. Straight lines
don't need anti-aliasing: they're axis-aligned.

* Nerd Font icons

Nerd Fonts put their icons in Unicode's Private Use Area (U+E000 to
U+F8FF and beyond), which has no official width, so =wcwidth= says 1.
Most icons are drawn wider than a cell. Your prompt prints three of them:
U+F314 (openSUSE), U+F115 (folder), U+F017 (clock). Cut to one cell, they
lost their edges.

guiltty does what Ghostty does:

- An icon followed by a plain space gets that space as room
  (=widen_icons= in [[file:../c_src/renderer/core/main.c][main.c]]): it's drawn into a two-cell area, and the
  space shows its right half. It stays centered on its own cell and only
  spills as far as it needs, so the gap the program meant after it is
  still there.
- Otherwise, or if it's wider than both cells, it's shrunk to fit
  instead of being cut. The same rule applies to any glyph from a fallback
  font whose ink would land outside its cells.

This happens only in the renderer. Elixir still sees an icon and a space,
so selection, search and the cursor work on the real cells.

* The atlas, filled as you go

The atlas ([[file:../c_src/renderer/core/atlas.c][atlas.c]]) is a 2048-pixel-wide RGBA bitmap cut into
cell-sized *slots* (186 per row at 11 px wide), up to 8,192 of them.
Slot 0 is always blank.

#+begin_example
   slot:  0     1     2     3     4     5     6 ...           185
         +-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+--   ...  --+-----+
   row 0 |     |  h  |  é  |  l  |  o  |  😀 (two slots) |     |
         +-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+--   ...  --+-----+
   row 1 | ...
#+end_example

A cache maps (cluster text, width) to a slot: an open-addressing hash
table, kept at most half full. A wide glyph takes two neighboring slots
in the same row, and its two cells name them: the =wide= cell slot =s=, its
spacer slot =s + 1=. So the shader still draws one cell per instance and
knows nothing about wide characters.

When the atlas is full, everything is thrown away: =generation= goes up,
and every cell on screen is looked up again (=refill_atlas=). The glyphs
in use come back; the ones that scrolled away don't. It's cheap, and rare:
8,192 different glyphs is a lot of screen.

** Uploading only what changed

Re-sending 2048x1035 RGBA (8 MB) for each new glyph would be wasteful.
The atlas tracks the band of pixel rows that changed since the last
upload (=dirty_top= to =dirty_bottom=); the GL backend sends just that band
with =glTexSubImage2D=. A new generation (or a new font) uploads the
whole texture with =glTexImage2D=.

* HiDPI and zoom

A HiDPI screen has more pixels per inch, and the desktop tells programs a
*content scale* (2.0 is common). SDL3 reports it with
=SDL_GetWindowDisplayScale=. The font size in your config is in points:
the renderer opens the font at =size x scale= pixels, and pads the grid by
=6 x scale=. =SDL_WINDOW_HIGH_PIXEL_DENSITY= asks for a full-resolution
drawable instead of one that's scaled up blurry.

Two units to keep apart: SDL sizes windows and reports mouse positions
in *window coordinates*, which on Wayland can be several pixels each
(=SDL_GetWindowPixelDensity=). The renderer converts mouse positions to
pixels before working out which cell they're in. When a window moves to a
screen with another scale (=SDL_EVENT_WINDOW_DISPLAY_SCALE_CHANGED=), the
font is opened again.

Zoom (Ctrl+= and Ctrl+-, Ctrl+0 to reset) is the same thing driven from
Elixir: the Window sends a FONT message with the size plus the zoom
steps, the renderer builds a new atlas, the cell size changes, and the
usual resize chain runs (chapter 9).

* Questions

1. Why check a font's charset after =FcFontSort= already ranked it?
2. Why does a wide glyph take two neighboring slots instead of one double
   slot?
3. What happens when the atlas fills up?
4. Why is 😀 shrunk, but a letter from the primary font isn't?
5. On a HiDPI screen with scale 2, what pixel size is a font of size 16?
6. Why does guiltty draw =─= itself instead of taking it from the font?
7. Where does the right half of a Nerd Font icon go when the next cell is
   a space?

** Answers
:PROPERTIES:
:VISIBILITY: folded
:END:

1. The sort ranks by everything in the pattern; coverage is one factor
   among many, not a guarantee.
2. Each cell names its own slot, so the shader draws one cell per
   instance as before; the spacer just names the slot after its left
   half's.
3. The cache and atlas are reset (a new generation), and every cell on
   screen is looked up again; the whole texture is uploaded.
4. The emoji comes from a bitmap font drawn at 109 pixels per em, far
   bigger than two cells. The primary font is opened at our pixel size, so its glyphs
   already fit.
5. 32 pixels.
6. A font's line rarely reaches exactly to the cell's edges and center,
   so neighbors leave gaps; drawn as whole pixels through the same center
   pixel in every cell, lines always meet.
7. Into that space's cell: the icon is drawn two cells wide and the space
   shows the second half (it still is a space for Elixir).
